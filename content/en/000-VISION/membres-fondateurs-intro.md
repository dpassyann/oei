## Joining the OEI at the moment it all begins

A movement is judged by who joins it before it has proven its success. That is the meaning of *founding member* status: it designates, in the precise sense of our governance, the people present **before the first broad public recognition** of the International Order of Information Technology Experts. It is neither the most prestigious status on paper, nor the most demanding one — it is the earliest. It precedes *accredited members*, who will one day meet competence and ethics criteria once our certification framework is operational; *associate members*, who support the mission without seeking accreditation; and *honorary members*, invited as a mark of honour. The founding member, by contrast, arrives when there is still only the vision, the White Paper, and work yet to be built.

### Why joining now has value

Joining the OEI today is not a passive gesture of support for an already-written cause. The organisation's body of work — code of ethics, competency framework, charters — remains largely to be built. Founding members are those whose review, critique and contribution carry the most weight, precisely because they step in before the texts are set in stone. Joining the OEI at this stage means taking part in defining what a recognised computer expert will be tomorrow — not merely benefiting from it once the work is finished by others.

### What this means in practice

Becoming a founding member means freely subscribing to the **Founding Member Charter**, a moral commitment rather than an enforceable legal obligation. Three commitments capture its spirit:

- **Respecting the truth about our status**: never presenting the OEI as a legally constituted professional order, and recalling that it is a founding movement resting on an association.
- **Contributing in good faith to the shared body of work** — through review, constructive critique, production or dissemination — in a spirit of cooperation and respect for peers.
- **Welcoming disagreement**: treating criticism and disagreement as raw material for the project, not as a threat.

To this are added broader commitments — serving the mission, letting quality prevail over posturing, defending the openness of the profession, embodying through one's own practice the ethical principles the OEI proposes to the profession as a whole. These commitments carry no binding legal value: they are commitments of honour, in the spirit that guided the founding of the movement.

The financial support detailed below, organised by contribution tiers, is one concrete way to express this commitment — it is neither its precondition nor its summary.
