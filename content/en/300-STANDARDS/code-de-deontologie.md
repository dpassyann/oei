# Code of Ethics of the Order of Computer Science Experts

## Preamble

This Code of Ethics sets out the rules of professional conduct that accredited members of the Order of Computer Science Experts (OEI) commit to upholding. It translates, into the daily practice of the profession, the OEI's mission as defined in our [vision and mission](/a-propos): to protect the public, to secure recognition for competence, and to raise the level of accountability within the computer science profession.

It is distinct from the **Charter of Commitment**, a less binding, voluntary text open to a wider audience (associate members, partner companies, academic institutions) who do not practise the profession individually. The present Code, by contrast, applies to every member accredited for the professional practice of computer science.

## Scope of application

This Code of Ethics applies to every accredited OEI member, regardless of employment status (employee, self-employed, executive, public service) or area of specialisation (development, cybersecurity, data, infrastructure, information systems governance, artificial intelligence, green IT, etc.). It applies to all of the member's professional activities relating to the design, development, operation, security or governance of digital systems.

## Principle 1 — Protection of the public

The member places the safety, privacy and legitimate interests of persons affected by their work above any other interest, whether commercial or hierarchical. Where they identify a serious risk to the safety of persons, data or critical infrastructure, they inform the relevant persons in charge and, where the situation warrants it and internal channels have been exhausted, the appropriate authorities or bodies.

## Principle 2 — Competence and continuing knowledge

The member practises only in areas where they possess genuine competence, acquired through training, experience, or a combination of both. They maintain and update their knowledge in step with the rapid evolution of technologies and threats, and acknowledge the limits of their expertise rather than concealing them.

## Principle 3 — Integrity and professional honesty

The member faithfully represents their qualifications, experience and the capabilities of the systems they design or assess. They neither conceal nor exaggerate the risks, technical limitations or uncertainties of a project to clients, employers or the public. They report errors of which they are aware, including their own, as soon as those errors are likely to have significant consequences.

## Principle 4 — Confidentiality and data protection

The member protects the confidentiality of information entrusted to or accessible by them in the course of their professional activity, except where legally required to disclose it or where there is a proven risk to the safety of others. In the design and operation of systems under their responsibility, they apply the principles of data minimisation and privacy by design.

## Principle 5 — Management of conflicts of interest

The member identifies and discloses any actual or apparent conflict of interest likely to affect their professional judgement or the interests of their clients, employers or the public. They refrain from deriving any improper personal advantage from their position, their technical access, or the information at their disposal.

## Principle 6 — Accountability and quality of work

The member takes responsibility for the systems they design, develop or operate, within the limits of their role and level of authority. They apply state-of-the-art practices (testing, review, documentation, vulnerability management) proportionate to the criticality of the system concerned, and refuse to yield to schedule or cost pressures that would seriously compromise the security or reliability of a high-stakes system.

## Principle 7 — Compliance with the law and cooperation with the Order's bodies

The member complies with the laws and regulations applicable to their activity in the jurisdictions where they practise. They cooperate in good faith with OEI's governance bodies in connection with verification, mediation or disciplinary inquiry procedures provided for under the organisation's statutes.

## Breaches and disciplinary procedure

Any proven breach of the principles set out in this Code may be reported to the OEI Governance Council. The Council examines each report on an adversarial basis, giving the member concerned the opportunity to present their observations before any decision is made. Depending on the severity and repeated nature of the breach, the measures that may be imposed range from a formal warning to the suspension or withdrawal of OEI accreditation. These measures will be set out in detail in the organisation's disciplinary regulations, currently being finalised by the Governance Council.

## Evolution of the Code

This Code of Ethics is a living document, reviewed periodically by the OEI Governance Council to take account of developments in technology, risk and society's expectations of the profession. Any substantial revision is communicated to accredited members before it takes effect.
