## A White Paper designed to be corrected

The OEI's White Paper is currently in version 3.1, and it will remain a work in progress for a long time, by methodological choice: we want it to be criticised, corrected and enriched before it is treated as settled. This document therefore explicitly calls for contributions from academics, information security officers, digital-law lawyers, engineers and field practitioners. Whether you disagree with our diagnosis, are reserved about our proposals, or are ready to contribute to them, your voice is useful to us.

### What we mean by "contributing"

Contributing to the OEI's body of work is not limited to joining as a member. It can take several forms, from the lightest to the most involved:

- a **critical review** of the White Paper or of a document currently in progress, flagging an inconsistency, an imprecision or a blind spot;
- a **targeted contribution of expertise** to one of the workstreams opened by the White Paper: the code of ethics, the competency and expertise-level framework, the independent certification framework, or the charters intended for members, companies and universities;
- a **reasoned opinion**, even a brief one, on a specific point of a workstream — there is no need to address the entire body of work for your contribution to count.

### What happens after you send your contribution

We want to be honest on this point rather than promise what we cannot deliver. Sending a contribution does not constitute a membership request, and does not trigger automatic membership in the movement. Our roadmap provides for the progressive assembly of a *first circle of credible contributors* — academics, security officers, technical management, digital-law lawyers — approached first for occasional opinions and reviews, never for immediate membership. It is in this spirit that we welcome your contribution: as an entry into dialogue, not as a membership application to be processed administratively.

The precise process for reviewing contributions — response timelines, follow-up arrangements, the form of our replies — remains to be clarified as this first circle takes shape. What we can guarantee today: every good-faith contribution will be read, and we welcome disagreement as raw material for the project, not as a threat.
